Unsettling isn't the same as loud. The performances that linger are usually the ones that treat menace as concentration — a man who has already decided what he will do, and is only waiting for the world to catch up. Three is enough. Padding a fourth would dilute the point.

## Javier Bardem in No Country for Old Men

Anton Chigurh is what happens when fate gets a haircut and a cattle gun. Bardem plays him with almost no interest in your fear — which is why the fear works. The coin toss isn't a quirk; it's theology with spare change. On ActorRating, this is a supporting performance that behaves like a weather system: everyone else is reacting to the pressure drop.

## Daniel Day-Lewis in There Will Be Blood

Daniel Plainview doesn't need a weapon. He needs a competitor and a pulpit. Day-Lewis builds a man whose ambition has eaten his ability to be around other people without turning them into fuel. The milkshake line is famous; the quieter cruelty is the craft. Unsettling because you understand him more than you want to.

## Christian Bale in American Psycho

Patrick Bateman is a void in a suit — and Bale commits to the void without winking early. The joke is horror; the horror is a joke; the performance never asks you to pick a lane. That instability is the unsettling part. Rate the control: voice, posture, the smile that doesn't reach anything behind the eyes.
