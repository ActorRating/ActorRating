ActorRating exists because a film's score and a performance's score are not the same number. Sometimes the movie is fine. Sometimes it's soft. And sometimes one actor is working so far above the surrounding material that treating them as the same rating feels like a category error.

This list starts from that split. Where we have Community Ratings, we use them against the film's Critic Aggregate (TMDB). Where the community hasn't weighed in yet, we still flag performances that clearly carry weaker films — because the pattern is the point.

## Rachel Weisz in The Mummy Returns

*The Mummy Returns* is a franchise victory lap with a Critic Aggregate in the mid-6s — perfectly watchable, rarely sharp. Weisz is the exception: she plays Evelyn like the movie still needs wit, not just sand and CGI. If you only remember The Rock wrestling the Scorpion King, go back and watch her hold the human center of a film that keeps trying to float away.

## Dwayne Johnson in The Mummy Returns

Johnson's Mathayus is pure star-engine — the film invents spectacle around him because it has to. That mid-6 Critic Aggregate is the movie. The Rock's presence is why people still quote it. Rate the performance on its own terms: physical comedy, mythic swagger, and a weirdly sincere commitment to a character who exists to sell a summer ride.

## Ryan Gosling in Barbie

Here's the data hook, not vibes. On ActorRating, Gosling's Ken has a Community Rating that sits clearly above Barbie's film Critic Aggregate. That's the thesis in one number: audiences are scoring the *turn* — the pathetic, funny, oddly sincere Ken — harder than the movie-as-product. If you felt the film was clever but Ken was the part that stuck, the community already said it out loud.

## Daniel Day-Lewis in There Will Be Blood

There Will Be Blood is not a mediocre movie — and that's useful. Day-Lewis's Community Rating still outruns a strong Critic Aggregate. When the best performance on the board beats a film critics already love, you're not "saving" a dud; you're watching acting pull gravity away from everything else on screen. Plainview doesn't share the frame. He owns it.

## Joaquin Phoenix in Joker

Same pattern, different temperature. Phoenix's Community Rating on ActorRating has cleared a film Critic Aggregate that already sits in awards territory. Whether you think *Joker* is cinema or provocation, the performance is the reason the argument exists. Score the Fleck, not the discourse.
