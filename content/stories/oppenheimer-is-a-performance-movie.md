People talk about *Oppenheimer* like it’s a physics theme park. The film is also a closed-circuit study of one face under pressure.

Murphy’s Oppenheimer barely raises his voice and still rearranges every room. Don’t let the runtime do your scoring for you.

## What the turn has to survive

Nolan surrounds Murphy with institutional noise: hearings, laboratories, parties, betrayals. The easy score inflates presence because the movie is loud around him. The honest score asks whether Murphy can make **listening** feel like action.

Watch the eyes when other people speak. Watch how ambition and guilt share a posture. Watch whether the “father of the bomb” mythology ever fully conquers the private man — or whether the private man keeps leaking through.

## Criteria that actually move

- **Believability** — genius without comic-book twitches
- **Emotional Range** — triumph that never gets clean
- **Technical Skill** — voice, stillness, aging across the cut
- **Presence** — not volume; gravitational attention
- **Chemistry** — with Blunt, Downey, the ensemble that keeps rewriting his moral weather

If your number is mostly “the trinity test slapped,” you graded the set piece. Rate Murphy. The IMAX will be fine without your charity.
