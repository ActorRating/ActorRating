You already know if *The Dark Knight* is “good.” That’s not the only interesting question.

The other question — the one most platforms flatten into the same star — is whether Heath Ledger’s Joker is a **9.2 performance** inside a crime epic, and whether Joaquin Phoenix’s Arthur Fleck is a different kind of high score in a different kind of machine.

**Don’t just rate the movie. Rate the acting as well.**

## Why both numbers matter

A film score asks: did the machine work?  
A performance score asks: did this human turn rewrite the room?

Those answers can diverge on purpose. A beloved movie can carry a soft lead. A messy movie can hide a career-best supporting dagger. If your platform cannot hold that contradiction, it is not a performance platform — it is a movie blog with face thumbnails.

## One slider or five knives

Hit **quick rate** when you want a gut number. Open the five criteria when you want to argue with yourself:

- Emotional Range
- Believability
- Technical Skill
- Screen Presence
- Chemistry

Ledger’s chaos is a technical and presence event. Phoenix’s descent is an emotional-range and believability event. Same mythic discourse lane. Two scorecards.

## How to practice

1. Rate the turn before you re-read the discourse.
2. Glance at the film-level signal if you want context — then keep it from rewriting your performance number.
3. Compare two performances of “the same idea” without demanding the films share a verdict.

Rate Ledger. Rate Phoenix. Keep the movie score. Add the acting score. That second number is the whole point of ActorRating.
