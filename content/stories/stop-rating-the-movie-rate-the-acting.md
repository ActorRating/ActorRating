You already know if *The Dark Knight* is “good.” That’s not the interesting question.

The interesting question is whether Heath Ledger’s Joker is a **9.2 performance** trapped inside a crime epic — and whether Joaquin Phoenix’s Arthur Fleck is a different kind of high score in a different kind of machine.

Most platforms smash those answers into one star. ActorRating splits them on purpose.

## Why the split matters

A film score asks: did the machine work?  
A performance score asks: did this human turn rewrite the room?

Those questions overlap. They are not identical. Ledger can be immortal inside a movie you have opinions about. Phoenix can be devastating inside a movie someone else calls uneven. If your platform cannot hold that contradiction, it is not a performance platform — it is a movie blog with face thumbnails.

## One slider or five knives

Hit **quick rate** when you want a gut number. Open the five criteria when you want to argue with yourself:

- Emotional Range
- Believability
- Technical Skill
- Screen Presence
- Chemistry

Ledger’s chaos is a technical and presence event. Phoenix’s descent is an emotional-range and believability event. Same mythic character-adjacent discourse. Two scorecards.

## How to practice

1. Rate the turn before you re-read the discourse.
2. If your number feels sticky, find the slider that is carrying secret movie baggage.
3. Compare two performances of “the same idea” without demanding the films share a verdict.

Rate Ledger. Rate Phoenix. Leave the Oscars and the timeline to their own sports.
