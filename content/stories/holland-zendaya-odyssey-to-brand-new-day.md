Some actors have a busy summer. Tom Holland and Zendaya are having a **genre collision**.

In Christopher Nolan’s *The Odyssey*, Holland plays Telemachus — a son hunting a father who became a story before he became a parent. Zendaya plays Athena — wisdom and warfare with a preferential attachment to Odysseus. Days later, the same two faces return as Peter Parker and MJ in *Spider-Man: Brand New Day* (wide release July 31), a film about being forgotten by the people who made you human.

That is not a publicity stunt. That is a two-film acting syllabus.

## The Odyssey: roles that refuse the Spider-Man muscle memory

Holland has spent a decade playing earnest competence under a mask. Telemachus is earnest competence **without** the mask — and without the comforting arc where swinging hard solves the emotional plot.

Telemachus is what happens when myth leaves a child unfinished. Rate whether Holland can play hunger without quipping. Rate whether “boy becoming man” looks like a Marvel second-act beat or like someone who has been performing adulthood for suitors since puberty.

Zendaya’s Athena is the sharper casting dare. She has already proven voltage (*Challengers*), political steel adjacent to soft power (*Dune*), and the long empathy game of MJ. Athena is not empathy first. Athena is **strategy that loves a mortal project**. If she plays the goddess like a love interest with better lighting, the theology of the poem goes soft. If she plays pure ice, you lose why Athena chooses Odysseus at all.

Their Odyssey work should feel older than their MCU work — not because the characters are “serious,” but because the emotions are less negotiable. Gods and sons do not get mid-credit reassurances.

## Brand New Day: the opposite problem

*Brand New Day* asks Holland to play Peter in a world that does not remember him — full-time Spider-Man, part-time person. That is a different loneliness from Telemachus. Telemachus is forgotten by a father. Peter is erased by a spell he asked for.

Zendaya’s MJ arrives with the cruelest acting assignment in modern franchise cinema: **move on convincingly** while the audience remembers the relationship she cannot. Chemistry here is not tennis-court voltage. It is the awkwardness of two people who almost share a private language and only one of them still has the dictionary.

If you rate Brand New Day like a theme-park ride, you will miss the performance thesis: what remains of a love story when one side has been edited out of continuity?

## Same actors, opposite scorecards

**Holland**
- *The Odyssey* — son seeking a myth
- *Brand New Day* — hero seeking a self

**Zendaya**
- *The Odyssey* — goddess who intervenes
- *Brand New Day* — woman who continued

**Core danger**
- *The Odyssey* — sentimentality / prestige cosplay
- *Brand New Day* — quip armor / trailer charm

**What to protect**
- *The Odyssey* — stillness, yearning, divine cool
- *Brand New Day* — grief without nostalgia bait

Do not average these into one “Holland 2026” vibe score. That is how platforms turn careers into soup.

## The question worth arguing about

Not “who had the better movie.”

Not “who won the weekend.”

This: **which performance required them to betray their most bankable instinct?**

Holland’s bankable instinct is likability. Zendaya’s is control. Odyssey and Brand New Day both punish easy likability and easy control — in different keys.

If your numbers cannot tell those keys apart, you are still rating the brand, not the acting.

Log the turns. The discourse will do the rest.
